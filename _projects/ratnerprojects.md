For the Ratner Lab, I researched polymer combinations to combine with different print geometries to hopefully continue pursuing the work of a previous PhD student in microporous scaffolds for dialysis access grafts. To do this, I used biocompatible polymers such as EGDMA, TPTMA, and HEMA, as well as the photoinitiator for cross-linking TPO, and the photoabsorber/light stabilizer BLS. Experimenting with different combinations and mass percentages of the polymers, I would create a standard 30g blend of each formulation and then "bake" some amount of them placed inside of a petri dish in a UV oven.

This blend turned out cracked and brittle. The goal for this research project was to arrive at an elastic yet firm biopolymer that could be printed with high resolution within our lab's SLA resin printer, similarly to the dental resin DS2000.

This is the lab's SLA resin printer which I was trained on, but unfortunately never used due to it being out of service — which is why my research was mostly focused on polymer blends and CAD.

Eventually, I started to get better blends that looked more elastic when incorporating a higher HEMA weight ratio, which I suspected was the main reason my earlier blends were failing.

I came to mostly the same conclusions on the blends as the original PhD student did, and found that a better blend would need to incorporate different polymers that he didn't use. Overall, it was a lot of fun to do research in the Ratner Lab and I interacted with and met a lot of cool people!
